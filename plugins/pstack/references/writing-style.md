# Writing preferences

Lead with the result or recommendation and explain the consequence. Use plain,
concrete language and stable terms. Connect the actor, action and reason; keep
technical detail only when it helps the reader decide or understand.

Preserve requested formats, quotations, code, uncertainty and material limits.
Use short paragraphs, with lists and tables when they make parallel information
easier to compare. Skip generic introductions, agent narration and repeated
conclusions. Do not force headings or templates onto a simple answer.

Support important claims with source or observed results. Distinguish proposed,
implemented and verified work. Give enough context that the reader can assess
the result without reconstructing the chat.
