How borrowing works

Four steps, one growing season

Borrowing seeds works like borrowing a book, except you return more than you took and nobody minds if you're late.

  1. 01

    Borrow

    Pick up to 10 packets per season at any branch. Each packet holds enough seed for a typical backyard bed.

    March to May
  2. 02

    Grow

    Plant them like any other seed. Every packet comes with a growing card written by a Portland gardener who has grown it here.

    Spring and summer
  3. 03

    Save

    Leave your healthiest plants to ripen fully, then dry and collect their seeds. We teach free seed-saving classes every August.

    Late summer
  4. 04

    Return

    Drop off labeled envelopes of saved seed at any branch. Returning is encouraged, never required, and failed crops are fine.

    September to November

    Questions

    Things people ask at the front desk

    Still curious? Stop by any branch or email hello@fernhillseeds.org.

    What happens if I don't return any seeds?

    Nothing at all. Crops fail, squirrels happen, and some plants are tricky to save from. You can still borrow next spring. Returns keep the collection growing, so we hope you'll try, but there are no fines or penalties.

    I've never saved seeds. Is this for me?

    Yes. Each packet is labeled with a saving difficulty, and beginners usually start with beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes, which are forgiving. Our August classes walk you through drying, cleaning and labeling.

    How much seed should I bring back?

    Aim for at least as much as you borrowed. A single tomato or bean plant usually gives far more than a packet's worth. Bring seeds in a paper envelope labeled with the variety name, the year and your branch.

    Why heirloom and open-pollinated seeds?

    Seeds from hybrid plants don't reliably grow into plants like their parent, so saving them doesn't work well. Open-pollinated varieties grow true from saved seed, and over a few seasons they adapt to Portland's wet springs and dry summers.

    Do I have to live in Portland?

    Anyone who gardens in the Portland metro area can join, including Beaverton, Gresham, Milwaukie and Vancouver, WA. Bring a photo ID or a piece of mail when you pick up your card.

    Can I donate seeds I bought or saved from another source?

    We gladly accept unopened, open-pollinated seed packets from the last two years, plus well-labeled home-saved seed. We can't accept hybrid (F1), treated or genetically modified seed.

    How can I help?

    Volunteers sort and package seeds on Saturday mornings from October to February, and we always need growers for our isolation gardens. Donations go directly to envelopes, classes and the branch seed cabinets.
