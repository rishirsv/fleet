Borrow seeds in spring. Bring some back in fall.

Fernhill Seed Library lends heirloom vegetable and flower seeds to Portland home gardeners, free. Grow them, let a few plants go to seed, and return what you save so next year’s neighbors can plant it too.

How borrowing works

JJan FFeb MMar AApr MMay JJun JJul AAug SSep OOct NNov DDec Borrow Grow Return Today

  1. March to May

    Borrow

    Visit any branch and choose the packets you’ll plant this year. Bring your member email; that’s your library card.

  2. June to August

    Grow

    Plant and harvest as usual, but leave a few of your healthiest plants unpicked so they can flower and set seed.

  3. September to November

    Save and return

    Dry the seed, label an envelope with the variety and year, and drop it at any branch. It goes back on the shelf for spring.

Questions

Does membership cost anything?

No. Membership is free, and so are the seeds. We’re a non-profit kept going by the seed members bring back each fall.

Do I have to return seeds?

We ask you to try. Seed returned in the fall is what stocks the shelves the next spring. If a crop fails or the season gets away from you, you’re still welcome to borrow again.

I’ve never saved seeds. Where do I start?

Pick varieties marked “Easy to save”, such as beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes. They pollinate themselves, so the seed you collect grows the same plant next year.

What does “Needs isolation” mean?

Insects carry pollen between plants, so a pepper or cucumber can cross with a neighbor’s different variety. To keep the seed true, grow only one variety of that crop, or cover a few blossoms with a mesh bag before they open.

How do I return seeds?

Let the seed dry completely, put it in a paper envelope labeled with the variety and the year you grew it, and drop it at any branch between September and November.

Can I donate seeds I didn’t borrow from you?

Yes, if they’re open-pollinated and you know the variety. We can’t take hybrid (F1) seed, because it won’t grow true to its parent.
