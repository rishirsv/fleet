Borrow seeds in spring. Bring some back in fall.

Fernhill lends heirloom vegetable and flower seeds to Portland gardeners for free. Grow them, let a few plants go to seed, and return what you save so next year's neighbors can grow them too.

How borrowing works

One loan follows one growing season. You don't need experience, a big yard or any money.

  1. Any time

    Get your card

    Sign up online or at a branch. Membership is free for anyone in the Portland area.

  2. March to May

    Pick your seeds

    Take up to 10 packets each spring. Volunteers at the branch can help you choose what suits your space.

  3. June to September

    Grow and save

    Eat most of your harvest. Leave a few of the healthiest plants to flower and set seed, then dry it indoors.

  4. September to November

    Bring some back

    Return a packet of saved seed for each one you borrowed. If a crop failed, tell us what happened. That helps too.

Questions

Still unsure? Ask a volunteer at any branch, or email hello@fernhillseeds.org.

Does it cost anything?

No. Membership and seeds are free. Packets, envelopes and workshops are paid for by donations and a few small grants.

What if my plants don't make seed?

Bring back what you can, or nothing at all. There are no fines. Let us know what went wrong when you visit, because knowing what struggles here helps us choose next year's seeds.

I've never saved seeds. Where do I start?

Choose packets marked Easy to save, like beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes. They pollinate themselves, so the seeds grow true to type. Every packet includes a short saving guide, and our summer workshops walk you through it.

Do I need a garden?

No. Bush beans, lettuce, poppies and many tomatoes grow well in containers on a balcony or porch. Volunteers can point you to varieties that suit small spaces.

How should I return seeds?

Dry the seeds completely, put them in a paper envelope and write the variety and the year you grew it. Drop it in the return box at any branch between September 1 and November 30.

Can I donate seeds I already have?

Yes, if they're open-pollinated and you know the variety. Bring them to any branch with a note about where they came from, and we'll start a new card for them.
