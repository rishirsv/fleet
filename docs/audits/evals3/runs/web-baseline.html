How borrowing works

One packet out, a handful back.

Every seed in the library was grown by a Portland gardener. Borrowing keeps these varieties alive and better suited to our wet springs and dry summers each year they’re grown here.

  1. March – May

    Borrow

    Pick up to 8 packets per season at any branch, or reserve online and collect them on your next visit.

  2. April – September

    Grow

    Plant, eat and enjoy. Each packet comes with a card on spacing and how to keep the variety true.

  3. August – October

    Save

    Leave a few of your best plants to mature, then dry and clean the seed. Our free workshops show you how.

  4. September – November

    Return

    Drop off your labeled envelope at any branch. We test it, package it and lend it out next spring.

Harvest didn’t make it? That’s fine. No fines, no late fees. Slugs, heat waves and a hungry raccoon are all part of gardening. Return what you can, when you can, and borrow again next year.

    Questions

    Good questions, honest answers.

    Ask anything else at any branch desk or write to hello@fernhillseeds.org.

    Is it really free?

    Yes. Membership, seeds and workshops are free. We’re a volunteer-run non-profit funded by donations and small grants from Portland neighborhood associations.

    What if I can’t return any seeds?

    You can still borrow next year. Returns keep the collection growing, but we never penalize anyone for a failed crop. Many members return seed every other year, and that’s plenty.

    I’ve never saved seeds. Is that a problem?

    Not at all. Start with varieties marked Easy to save, like beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes. They mostly self-pollinate, so the seed comes true without special isolation. Our August workshops cover the rest.

    How much seed should I bring back?

    Aim for roughly twice what you borrowed, so the library grows each year. A coin envelope labeled with the variety name, the year and your branch is perfect. We provide envelopes at every branch.

    Why heirlooms and not hybrids?

    Heirlooms are open-pollinated, which means seed saved from them grows into plants like the parent. Seed saved from F1 hybrids usually doesn’t, so they can’t be passed along the same way.

    Do I need to live in Portland?

    Anyone in the greater Portland area is welcome, including Vancouver, Beaverton and Gresham. Our seeds are adapted to the Willamette Valley, so they do best close to home.

    Can I donate seeds I didn’t borrow from you?

    Yes, if they’re open-pollinated and you know the variety. Bring them to any branch with a note on where they came from and how long you’ve grown them. Family seeds with a story are especially welcome.
