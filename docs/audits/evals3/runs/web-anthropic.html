Borrow seeds in spring. Bring seeds back in fall.

We lend heirloom vegetable and flower seeds to Portland home gardeners for free. Grow them, save seeds from your best plants, and return some so the next gardener can grow them too.

How borrowing works

A loan lasts one growing season. There are no fees, and no penalty if a crop doesn't make it.

  1. Any time

    Get a card

    Sign up below or at any branch. Membership is free for anyone who gardens in the Portland area.

  2. March to May

    Borrow up to 5 packets

    Browse the seed drawers at a branch, or reserve packets online and pick them up.

  3. May to September

    Grow and let some go to seed

    Leave a few of your healthiest plants in the ground until the seeds are ripe and dry.

  4. September to November

    Return saved seeds

    Put dry seeds in an envelope, write the variety and your neighborhood on it, and drop it in any branch's return box.

    Common questions

    Does it cost anything?

    No. Membership, seeds and workshops are free. We're a non-profit funded by donations and a small city grant, and there are never late fees.

    What if my plants don't produce seeds?

    It happens to everyone. Slugs, a cold June or an early frost can end a crop. Let us know when you visit and borrow again next spring.

    Do I have to return the same variety I borrowed?

    We'd love that, since it keeps each variety in circulation. If it didn't work out, you can return any open-pollinated seeds you saved from your garden instead.

    Which seeds are easiest to save if I'm new?

    Beans, peas, lettuce and tomatoes. They mostly pollinate themselves, so the seeds come out true even if a neighbor grows a different variety. Look for "Easy" on the packet.

    How many seeds should I bring back?

    Enough to fill at least one packet, usually 20 to 30 seeds. Keep the rest for your own garden or share them with friends.

    Can I donate seeds I didn't borrow from you?

    Yes, if they're open-pollinated and saved within the last two years. Label the envelope with the variety, the year and where it grew. We can't accept hybrid (F1) seeds because they don't grow true.
