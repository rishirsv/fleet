Fernhill lends heirloom vegetable and flower seeds to Portland gardeners for free. Grow them, let your best plants go to seed, and return a share so next year’s gardeners can grow them too.

How borrowing works

One packet follows the garden year. You take it home in March and bring seed back once the plants are done.

March

Borrow up to six packets

Pick them up on lending day or any open hour after. Each packet holds enough seed for a home bed.

April to August

Grow them like any garden seed

Each packet has planting dates for the Willamette Valley. Ask a volunteer at any branch if you’re unsure.

August to October

Let your best plants go to seed

Leave a few of the healthiest plants unpicked until their seed is dry. Tomatoes, beans and lettuce are the easiest place to start.

September to November

Return a share of your seed

Bring at least as much as you borrowed, dry and labeled with the variety. Envelopes are free at every branch.

    Common questions

    Does it cost anything?

    No. Membership and seeds are both free. Donations help us buy envelopes and replace varieties that had a hard year, but nobody asks at checkout.

    What if my plants fail and I have nothing to return?

    Then you return nothing, and you can still borrow next spring. Slugs, heat waves and late frosts happen to everyone. Tell a volunteer what went wrong and we’ll suggest something hardier.

    How much seed should I bring back?

    At least as much as you borrowed, more if you can. Dry the seed fully, put it in a paper envelope and write the variety and the year on it. Plastic bags trap moisture and spoil seed.

    I’ve never saved seed. Where do I start?

    Pick seeds marked “Easy to save” in the catalog. Tomatoes, beans, peas and lettuce pollinate themselves, so the seed grows the same plant next year without any special care.

    Our free workshops in July and September walk through harvesting and drying.

    Can I donate seed from my own garden?

    Yes, if it’s open-pollinated. Seed from hybrids, often labeled F1 on the packet, won’t grow the same plant again. Bring it to any branch with the variety name and where you grew it.

    Do I need to live in Portland?

    Anyone can join. Our seeds are chosen and grown for the Willamette Valley, so they do best within about an hour’s drive.

    I only have a balcony. Is that enough?

    Yes. Stupice tomato, Black Seeded Simpson lettuce, dill and calendula all grow well in pots of 5 gallons or more.
